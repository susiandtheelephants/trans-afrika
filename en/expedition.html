North Africa & Sahara

Morocco is a gentle start. Souks, the Atlas mountains and the first off-road sections in the Sahara. Then south along the Atlantic coast through Western Sahara. In Mauritania it gets serious: sand, heat and long, empty stretches. We sleep between the dunes, pick out constellations in the night sky and cook over an open fire.

West Africa

Senegal changes everything: more life, more colour, more chaos. In The Gambia we follow the Gambia River deep inland and, with a bit of luck, see chimpanzees. Guinea is one of the least developed countries on the whole route: potholes, red pistes, small villages and untouched waterfalls.

Gulf of Guinea

Beaches, tropical rainforest and loud, restless cities take turns. In Ghana, Togo and Benin we get into the cultural range of the region: local markets, voodoo, and the colonial history that follows us eastwards. Nigeria is one of the most intense stretches of the whole trip: loud, chaotic and full of energy.

Central Africa

We reach Cameroon after days on off-road tracks in the Nigerian mountains. In the Republic of the Congo we look for chimpanzees and gorillas before catching our breath in Brazzaville. DR Congo has the hardest and perhaps the finest off-road section of the whole trip.

Angola & Namibia

In Angola the landscape opens up. Wide savannah, remote coastline, long horizons. Namibia begins the calmer part: campsites, lodges and more infrastructure, but also wildlife and some of the best scenery on the route.

South Africa

The last kilometres take us into South Africa. We reach Cape Town, the southernmost point of this trip and one of the most beautiful places in the world.

FAQ

Common questions.

The things we are asked most often.

What does the expedition cost?

Expected from 14,990 euros per person, based on two people per vehicle. If you travel alone in a vehicle there is a supplement of 7,990 euros, because the vehicle related costs on our side are not shared. The price includes expedition leadership, organisation, daily route guidance, hotels, campsites, tours, satellite internet, radios and the meet-the-group weekend before departure. It does not include fuel, visas, food, insurance or your flight home.

What additional costs should I expect?

The biggest items are fuel (roughly 3,000 to 4,000 euros depending on your vehicle) and visas (roughly 1,500 euros per person). Food varies a lot: shopping at local markets is cheap, while imported goods in the capitals can cost more than they do in Europe.

Do I need my own vehicle?

Yes. You drive your own 4x4. Johnny leads the convoy and helps when something breaks, but everyone drives themselves. For tyres we recommend all terrain.

Can I join on my own?

Yes, of course. You do not have to come as a couple. The group is deliberately small, six vehicles at most, and you all meet each other at the weekend before departure.

Why the west route?

The west route is currently the only way to drive right through Africa from north to south. Every country on it is passable. It is also the most interesting route, with the most countries and the most cultures along the way.

Do I need off-road experience?

No. You learn it on the way. We start on easy pistes and build up step by step, until the group handles the difficult sections too. Johnny drives ahead and knows every passage.

Which vaccinations do I need?

We recommend an appointment at a tropical medicine institute, in our case the one in Hamburg. Yellow fever vaccination is mandatory in many West African countries. Beyond that, hepatitis A and B, tetanus, typhoid and polio are recommended. There is no vaccine against malaria; protection comes from prophylaxis such as Malarone and from being consistent about mosquito protection.

What do I need to bring?

We provide a detailed packing list: vehicle equipment, tools, provisions, your own medical kit and the small things that turn out to be useful. You do not have to work it out on your own.

What does a typical day look like?

We set off after breakfast and drive five to six days a week on average. We reach the camp spot in the afternoon, which leaves time to look around, cook and do nothing. In between there are rest days for visas, vehicle maintenance, recovery and whatever there is to see.

How dangerous is the trip?

This trip goes through areas with no tourist infrastructure. We plan carefully, we know the route from our own experience, we carry satellite internet, GPS and a comprehensive medical kit, and we know the hospitals and clinics along the way. Risk can never be ruled out completely, but with experience and preparation it can be managed well.

How hard is the trip, really?

It is hard, at times. A new environment every few days, a lot to take in, temperatures that swing, nowhere to retreat to, curious people everywhere, border crossings and police checks. This is not an easy holiday. It is an expedition.

What happens if my vehicle breaks down?

We check the vehicles regularly and deal with problems before they grow. If something does break, we get the vehicle to the nearest garage and have it repaired there. Johnny knows garages along the route, and the group helps each other out.

What if I do not get on with the group?

That is what the weekend before departure is for. You meet each other there. The group is small, and wanting to drive across Africa is a lot to have in common.

Can I leave the trip partway through?

That is not the plan. If it becomes necessary, it is possible. There are international airports in the larger cities with flights to Paris, Brussels, London or Frankfurt. Your vehicle can be stored safely with selected partners or shipped home.

How do I ship my vehicle back to Europe?

We work with an experienced shipping agent in Cape Town who sends the vehicles to Rotterdam by container, safely and at competitive rates. You can also leave your vehicle there and have it shipped at a later date.

How does a border crossing work?

Every border is different. Usually you check out of one country and into the next, people on the passport and the vehicle on the Carnet de Passages. Johnny knows all the crossings on the route and handles the organisation.

What are the booking and cancellation terms?

These terms apply from the opening of bookings for 2027/28. The waiting list is free and commits you to nothing. A place is reserved with a deposit of 30 per cent. The balance is due 90 days before departure. The expedition runs with a minimum of 4 vehicles and a maximum of 6. If the minimum is not reached, we reserve the right to withdraw up to 120 days before departure. In that case every payment made is refunded in full.

If you cancel, the following applies: up to 120 days before departure the deposit is forfeited. From 119 to 60 days, 50 per cent is due; from 59 to 30 days, 75 per cent; from 29 days, the full price. Cancellation insurance is therefore strongly recommended. An expedition like this takes months of preparation for everyone involved, which is why late cancellations are not possible.
